\section{Abstract}

In this seminar paper, we will study the links between trading in the S\&P 500 index and searches for the terms "Trump" and "stocks" on Google. The S\&P 500 index consists of the 500 most valuable stocks actively traded in the United States, and American politics often has a major influence on its value.

We will investigate the relationship of hml (the difference between the highest and lowest prices during the day, \textit{high minus low}), change (the closing price minus the opening price during the day), and volume (the quantity of the index traded during the day) with the relative frequencies of searches for these terms on Google. Using descriptive statistics, we will examine the distributions of these quantities and formulate hypotheses based on them.

We will then use linear regression based on the frequency of searches for the keywords to estimate certain indicators of the S\&P 500 index.
We will also test the hypothesis that the distribution of HML is log-normal.
This analysis will establish the nature of the relationship between trading in the S\&P 500 index and the use of the search engine.
